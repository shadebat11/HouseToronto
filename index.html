Toronto housing affordability — explained

Toronto's housing market is one of the most expensive in North America. The median home price is now over $1.0M, while condos typically sit in the high $600Ks. Since 2015, the gap between what households earn and what they need to qualify for a mortgage has grown significantly. This page is designed to give you a quick, realistic snapshot of where you stand: by age group, region, and across different interest-rate scenarios.

How the calculators work

Across this page, the estimated qualifying income uses a simplified benchmark: about 22.5% of the home price as required gross household income per year. This is based on a typical 30% Gross Debt Service (GDS) ratio using common Toronto mortgage assumptions.

Mortgage payment estimates assume:

  • 20% down payment (80% loan-to-value)
  • 25-year amortization
  • The interest rate selected in the global rate slider at the top of the page

The mortgage stress test calculator uses OSFI's B-20 qualification rule, which requires lenders to test borrowers at the higher of:

  • your contract mortgage rate plus 2%, or
  • 5.25%

This means the qualifying payment shown there reflects what lenders will actually use to assess affordability, even if your real monthly payment would be lower.

How to read the income percentile data

Income figures are based on Statistics Canada T1FF microdata summaries for the Toronto CMA (FY2024). The income distribution chart at the top of the page allows you to switch between:

  • Upper-income range (P90–P99)
  • Full distribution (P10–P99)

This gives you a quick view of how incomes are spread across households, and how inequality changes within each age or regional group.

Common questions

What income do I need to buy a house in Toronto?

For an average detached or semi-detached home at around $1.13M, you'd need a qualifying household income of roughly $250,000 at today's rates. That's well above Toronto's median household income of about $100,500. For a condo at $651K, the threshold drops to about $149,000. These numbers move when mortgage rates change. Adjust the rate slider at the top of the page to see how.

What is the FHSA and how does it help with a Toronto downpayment?

The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) lets eligible first-time buyers contribute up to $8,000 per year (lifetime cap of $40,000) on a tax-deductible basis, then withdraw the funds tax-free when buying a qualifying home. Combined with the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan ($60,000 per person as of 2024), a couple can put up to $200,000 of tax-advantaged savings toward a downpayment. For a $651K Toronto condo with 20% down ($130,200), that's enough to cover the full downpayment.

How does the mortgage stress test work in 2024–2025?

Federally regulated lenders have to qualify uninsured borrowers at the higher of your contract rate plus 2%, or 5.25%. With five-year fixed rates currently sitting around 5.0–5.5%, the qualifying rate is typically 7.0–7.5%. In plain terms: even though your actual monthly payment is based on the lower contract rate, the bank decides whether you "qualify" using the higher stress-tested payment. The stress test calculator on this page lets you see how this affects your numbers.

Why does my income percentile change when I switch age cohorts?

Income tends to peak in the 45–54 age group, then decline through retirement. So a $100,000 income puts a 25–34-year-old around the 80th percentile, but a 45–54-year-old closer to the 60th. This is why housing affordability hits younger Torontonians hardest. They face the same qualifying income thresholds, but the income distribution in their cohort sits much further below them.

How long will it take me to save a downpayment?

The downpayment savings runway section above shows how many years it would take at 10%, 20%, or 30% savings rates of your gross income (set by the income slider). At a $100,000 income with a 20% savings rate ($20,000/yr), the minimum downpayment for an average condo (~$40,000) takes about two years to save. A 20% downpayment ($130,000) takes closer to seven. These numbers don't include investment returns on your savings, which can shorten the timeline noticeably.

Methodology & sources

  • Income distributions: Statistics Canada T1FF custom tabulations, FY2024, Toronto CMA
  • Housing prices (city-wide): CMHC Housing Market Information Portal, Toronto CMA, 2024
  • Regional pricing: Toronto Regional Real Estate Board (TRREB) Q2 2025 market watch
  • Stress test rule: OSFI Guideline B-20
  • FHSA / RRSP HBP figures: Canada Revenue Agency, current as of 2024

The methodology is intentionally simplified for transparency. Property tax, condo fees, insurance, and CMHC mortgage insurance premiums on insured mortgages aren't separately modelled in every calculator on this page. See the per-tool notes.
